# Chapter 1 — Analyzing Business Domains

## Key Ideas
- **Understand the business domain** — know which part of a client's business the software supports; the product being built may not be the business's core offering.
- **Distinguish subdomain types** — generic subdomains solve common problems, supporting subdomains help the business operate, and core subdomains are where complexity and competitive advantage can concentrate.
- **Invest in core subdomains** — these solve challenging, usually complex problems that are hard for competitors to imitate; put your strongest engineers and engineering tools here.
- **Keep the competitive edge** — core subdomains change frequently as the business works to stay ahead, so expect ongoing development rather than a finished solution.
- **Keep supporting solutions simple** — supporting subdomains are usually less complex and custom-built, serving the core without providing a competitive edge themselves; use a simpler implementation, an available solution, or outsourced development where appropriate.
- **Reuse generic solutions** — an online shop for a jewelry business may be a common capability rather than what differentiates that business, so it need not be reinvented from scratch.
- **Separate common from easy** — generic subdomains such as authentication and encryption can be complex, but their problems are widely shared and often have commercial or open-source solutions.
- **Match engineering effort to the subdomain** — reserve the strongest expertise for core work; less experienced engineers can contribute to supporting work and the integration of established generic solutions.
- **Expect domains to evolve** — a business can have multiple domains, and what counts as its core can shift as it grows or competitors imitate its earlier advantages.
- **Collaborate with domain experts** — the people who deeply understand the business are usually not engineers; stay in touch with them to learn the domain and discuss proposed solutions throughout development.

## Core Message
> Understanding how a business's subdomains contribute to its goals helps engineers focus custom effort on what differentiates it and reuse common solutions elsewhere.
